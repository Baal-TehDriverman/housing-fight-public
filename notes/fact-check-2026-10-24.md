# PART 2 — CORRECTIONS TO MY OWN EARLIER CLAIMS

Four things I got wrong. They are corrected here rather than left in the notes.

## 2.1 The pay-or-vacate date discrepancy is NOT a defect

**I claimed the notice was internally inconsistent and implied a defect.** Wrong.

The notice is dated **September 24, 2026** and demands payment "on or before, and
not later than **10/17/2026**, **Fourteen (14)** days henceforth."

Fourteen days from September 24 is **October 8**. The notice gives **October 17**
— twenty-three days.

**A math discrepancy that gives the tenant MORE time than the statute requires is
not a defect. It means the notice is generous.** The correct framing: the notice
grants twenty-three days against a fourteen-day minimum, and that is favourable.
Two readings, neither harmful:
- the printed deadline controls and the tenant has until 10/17; or
- the notice was served later than its own date line, making 14 days land on 10/17.

**Do not plead this as a defect.** It would be corrected in front of a reviewer
and cost credibility on the parts of the notice that are genuinely attackable.

## 2.2 The 2%/3%/5% late-fee caps may not reach the original lease term

**RCW 59.20.060(2)(j), verbatim:**

> "(j) Allowing the landlord to charge a late fee for rent that is paid within
> five days following its due date **for leases or rental agreements entered into
> or renewed on or after May 7, 2025**. If rent is more than five days past due,
> the landlord may charge late fees commencing from the first day after the due
> date until paid. During the first month that rent is past due, late fees may not
> exceed two percent of the tenant's total rent per month. During the second
> consecutive month... three percent... During the third consecutive month and
> all subsequent consecutive months... five percent..."

**The caps are keyed to a date.** The lease was signed **November 4, 2024** —
before May 7, 2025.

**The argument that saves it:** the lease is **month-to-month** and RCW
59.20.090(1) provides that a rental agreement "shall be automatically renewed for
the term of the original rental agreement." A month-to-month tenancy renews
monthly, so every renewal from May 2025 onward is a renewal "on or after May 7,
2025."

**That is an argument, not a certainty.** Plead it, but do not rest the case on
it.

## 2.3 The rent-increase notice violation is NOT established

**I was leaning toward a three-month notice violation.** The evidence does not
support it.

**RCW 59.20.090(2), verbatim:**

> "(2) A landlord seeking to increase the rent upon expiration of the term of a
> rental agreement **of any duration** shall notify the tenant in writing **three
> months prior** to the effective date of any increase in rent."

The increase is effective **11/1/2026**. The exemptions page of the same form
carries the date line **"DATED this 10 of April, 2026"**, signed Kathleen
Schrier, LANDLORD / AUTHORIZED AGENT.

**April 10 to November 1 is roughly seven months** — well past three months. On
the document's own date, the notice is compliant.

**What is unverified:** the actual **service date**. The file is named
`2026-09-25-...`, which may record when it was photographed rather than when it
was dated or served. The signature block on page 1 is cut off in the image, so
the form's own date line for page 1 is not in hand.

**Do not plead a notice-period violation until the service date is established.**
If it turns out the notice was served in September 2026, the arithmetic changes
completely and the violation is clean — but it is not established today.

## 2.4 The 1.5% admission — still the strongest written concession

The landlord's letter says late fees are "capped at 1.5% of the rent amount."
That is not the statute (2/3/5%), and the lease says $20–$60.

**The lease's own savings clause, §5:**

> "...unless otherwise defined by local governance, in which **the more
> restrictive late fee will take precedence**."

Three figures. Lease $60. Statute $17 (month one). Their letter $12.75.
**The lowest governs, and the landlord wrote it.**

**One caution the statute adds:** RCW 59.20.060(2)(j) bars any late fee "for rent
that is paid within five days following its due date." The statement's own note
says "Your payment is due by the 1st, with a grace period per your individual
lease. Standard grace periods have been extended to midnight on the 8th." Rent
paid on the 5th or 7th is **within five days of the 1st** — no late fee is
chargeable at all under (2)(j) if the caps apply.

# PART 3 — THE MONEY-BACK PATHS, RANKED

## 3.1 RCW 59.30.040(7)(a) — administrative refund order · LOT side

**Verbatim:**

> "The attorney general may issue an order requiring the respondent... to cease
> and desist from an unlawful practice and take affirmative actions... The
> affirmative actions may include... **(a) Refunds of rent increases, improper
> fees, charges, and assessments** collected in violation of this chapter."

**This is a refund order obtained administratively, without filing suit.** It is
the lot-side money path, and it is already live — the MHDRP investigation is
open on files 739331 and 739578.

**What it can reach:** improper fees, charges, assessments, rent increases
collected in violation of RCW 59.20. **What it cannot reach:** the Ron'Cal home
contract. MHDRP enforces the tenancy chapter against the lot landlord.

## 3.2 RCW 19.86.090 — private action · CONTRACT side

**Verbatim:**

> "...may bring a civil action in superior court to enjoin further violations, to
> recover the actual damages sustained by him or her, or both, together with the
> costs of the suit, including a reasonable attorney's fee. In addition, the court
> may, in its discretion, increase the award of damages up to an amount not to
> exceed three times the actual damages sustained: **PROVIDED, That such increased
> damage award for violation of RCW 19.86.020 may not exceed twenty-five thousand
> dollars**..."

**Read the cap carefully — it is on the increase, not the total.**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Actual damages (documented payments into the contract) | ~$41,265 |
| Increased award — up to 3× actual, **capped at $25,000** | up to $25,000 |
| Costs and reasonable attorney's fee | fee-shifting |
| **Potential total** | **up to ~$66,265 + fees** |

**The treble is capped at $25,000, so fee-shifting is the real prize.** The
attorney's fee is uncapped and is what makes a contingency lawyer take the case.

**This is a private action the consumer files.** An administrative complaint
pays no treble. Say that plainly before building a plan around a check.

**The theory is RCW 19.86.020, verbatim:**

> "Unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the
> conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful."

## 3.3 RCW 59.20.370(4) — rent-cap enforcement

> "(4)(a) A tenant or the attorney general may bring an action... on a finding of
> violation the court awards (i) excess rent/fees paid, (ii) up to three months of
> unlawful rent or fees, (iii) reasonable attorneys' fees and costs. (4)(b) the AG
> may sue without regard to whether the tenant offered a cure and may recover
> civil penalties up to $7,500 per violation."

**Keyed to a finding of violation.** A 3% increase is not above the 5% cap, so
(4)(b)'s civil penalties are not available on the percentage alone.

**Do NOT plead the cure-and-terminate right.** RCW 59.20.370(3) keys to an
increase "above the amount allowed in subsection (1)." Three percent is not
above five percent. Asserting it invites a clean denial.

## 3.4 RCW 19.86.140 — AG penalties

Civil penalty up to **$7,500 per violation** of RCW 19.86.020; up to $125,000 for
violating an injunction. **This is the State's money, not his.** It is leverage,
not a recovery.

## 3.5 Route each claim to the instrument that reaches it

| Claim | Instrument | Forum |
|---|---|---|
| Improper fees, water charges, notice defects | RCW 59.20 | MHDRP (administrative) |
| Refund of improper fees/charges | RCW 59.30.040(7)(a) | MHDRP |
| Rent-increase cap | RCW 59.20.370 | Court or AG |
| Deceptive contract, no down payment, no credit | RCW 19.86.020 / .090 | Superior court, private |
| Unconscionability of the sale | RCW 19.86.030 / UCC 2-302 | Superior court, private |

**Never bundle the contract claim into the MHDRP filing.** Different statute,
different respondent, different relief — and a closure on the out-of-scope half
can take the in-scope half with it.

# PART 4 — GETTING OUT: THE FORFEITURE WARNING

**This is the most important section in this document. Read it before any move.**

## 4.1 Walking away forfeits every dollar

The contract says so twice:

> **§7(b)** "The Five Thousand Dollars... in consideration for the grant of this
> option **does not constitute a down payment and is non-refundable upon
> execution of this Lease.**"

> **§7(c)** "**No credit shall be given for the consideration paid for the option
> by Lessee or for Lease payments received by the Lessor.**"

**He has paid approximately $41,265 into this arrangement.** Under those two
clauses, a voluntary exit returns **$0** and builds no equity. That is not a
reason to stay — it is the reason the exit has to be negotiated or litigated,
not simply walked.

## 4.2 The 120-day sale right attaches to the INVOLUNTARY path

**RCW 59.20.080(3):**

> "...shall be allowed **120 days** within which to sell the tenant's mobile home
>... **in place**," conditioned on remaining current on post-eviction rent and
> paying "any past due rent, reasonable attorneys' fees and court costs **at the
> time the rental agreement is assigned**."

**A voluntary surrender does not trigger it and may forfeit it.** The right runs
from an eviction, not from a decision to leave.

**And the assignment condition is where the unitemized arrears demand pays off:**
"past due rent" at assignment is a number nobody has proven. **Require an
itemized ledger and a written receipt before agreeing to any figure there.**

## 4.3 The lot tenancy is month-to-month — that exit is cheap

**RCW 59.20.090(3), verbatim:**

> "(3) A tenant shall notify the landlord in writing **one month prior** to the
> expiration of a rental agreement of an intention not to renew."

Because the lease is month-to-month, the tenant gives **one month's written
notice** to end the lot tenancy. **Ending the lot lease does not end the home
contract** — they are separate obligations against different parties.

**The two obligations are separable, and the values are very different:**

| Obligation | Party | Monthly | Exit cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lot lease | Cedar Lane Mobile Estates LLC | $850 | One month's notice |
| Home contract | Ron'Cal Homes LLC | $705 | **Forfeits ~$41,265** |

## 4.4 Rescission is the remedy that matches the goal

"I want out of this deceptive contract" is a **rescission** request, and it is an
equitable remedy for deceptive inducement. RCW 19.86.090 expressly authorizes
"to **enjoin further violations**, to recover the actual damages" — rescission
sits alongside that as equitable relief in the same action.

**The record already supports it:**
- The operator's own pre-screening form says "buying on contract"
- The lender wrote "this home purchase" and named the seller-financing route
- The agent wrote the refinance advice three days later
- The executed contract says the $5,000 is not a down payment and no credit is
  given for any of it
- The assessor lists the seller as owner

**The claim does not require a document where the landlord says "you own this."
It requires the landlord's own documents to contradict each other** — and they
do, in writing, from three independent sources.

## 4.5 The defense if they file first

**RCW 59.20.240, verbatim proviso:**

> "PROVIDED, That this section shall not be construed as limiting the tenant's
> right in an unlawful detainer proceeding to raise the defense that **there is no
> rent due and owing**."

**RCW 59.20.080(1)(b)** permits a 14-day notice only for "rent or other charges
**specified in the rental agreement**." Every dollar of a demanded balance must
trace to the signed lease.

**RCW 59.20.134(1)** — the landlord "must accept a personal check, cashier's
check, or money order." The notice demanded "money order, or cashier's check
only." No bounced check is in the record. **Ask for a personal check in writing
and record the answer — the refusal is the violation.**

# THE BOTTOM LINE

**Money back:** two lanes. The lot side runs through MHDRP and RCW 59.30.040(7)(a)
— administrative, no suit, already open. The contract side runs through RCW
19.86.020/.090 — private action, actual damages plus a treble capped at $25,000,
plus uncapped attorney's fees.

**Out:** the lot tenancy is month-to-month and ends on one month's notice. The
home contract is the one that cannot be walked away from — §7(b) and §7(c) make a
voluntary exit a total forfeiture of roughly $41,265. The exit from the contract
is **rescission**, sought as equitable relief in the same action that claims the
deception, not a surrender.

**Do not surrender. Do not stop paying the lot rent while the dispute runs. Do
not agree to any arrears figure without an itemized ledger and a receipt.**
