## The down payment question — what the numbers do and do not show

**Stated figures:**

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Lender required | 5% down — Vandenhaak, Oct 5 |
| Eric disclosed | "I do have 9-10%" — his reply, Oct 5 |
| Contract consideration | $5,000 — §7(b) |
| Advance Payment | $500 — sample terms, Oct 10 |
| Paid | $5,500 — Schrier's email, Oct 30 |
| Home assessed value | $56,000 — Skagit parcel P136913 |
| Contract purchase price | $25,000 — §7(c) |

**Computed (label as computed, not fact):**

```
5% of $55,000  = $2,750    ← what the lender actually needed
9% of $55,000  = $4,950    ← bottom of what he told them
10% of $55,000 = $5,500    ← what he paid   ← exact match
5% of $25,000  = $1,250
22% of $25,000 = $5,500    ← as a share of the contract price
```

**The $500 gap is explained.** The sample terms Igor Oliveira sent on Oct 10
listed two signing payments:

> "Consideration payment due at signing ($5,000.00)
> Advance Payment due at signing ($500.00)
>   - This payment will be applied to the end of the contract and reduce final
>     cashout amounts."

$5,000 + $500 = $5,500. That is the whole gap. It is a second line item, not
unexplained money.

**What this does NOT prove.** The "sizing to disclosed liquidity" observation —
that the amount was set to the ceiling of what he volunteered — is an
**inference, not a documented fact.** The $5,000 is a stated term of the deal,
and the sample terms did say the upfront "can be negotiated." The observation
is worth recording and worth raising, but it must be labeled as computed and
not hardened into an assertion of intent.
