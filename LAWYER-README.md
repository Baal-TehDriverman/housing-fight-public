## 5. The claims, graded honestly

### Strong — documents carry these

1. **Payment-method restriction vs. RCW 59.20.134(1).** The notice
   demands "money order, or cashier's check only." The statute requires
   a landlord to accept a personal check, cashier's check, or money
   order, except a personal check from a tenant who bounced one to the
   landlord/agent within the previous nine months. No bounced check is
   in the record. The October statements say "checks/money orders (NO
   CASH)" — a different, broader instruction than the notice's. This is
   a real, document-based notice defect. (Statute text independently
   verified in this review.)
2. **Billing-method contradiction (three documents, three methods).**
   Lot lease §9 (per the repo decode): utilities not sub-metered are
   billed by RUBS — "dividing its actual utility costs evenly among
   each occupied space." Landlord's Oct 24 letter: "uses your total
   consumption to calculate your percentage of the respective bill."
   October statement: meter readings and a consumption-derived water
   charge ($19.31 for 1,494 gal). The lease method and the practiced
   method cannot both be right. Note the dollar direction: $19.31 is
   BELOW an even share of the community PUD bills (~$2,219–$2,691/mo
   ÷ 47 lots ≈ $47–$57), so this is a transparency/contract-compliance
   point, not a demonstrated overcharge — and the PUD master bills
   provided cover Apr–Jun 2025 only, not the disputed months, with no
   formula linking them to Lot 24's charge. Do not plead a water
   overcharge amount without the allocation schedule.
3. **The lease-option structure vs. the "buying" framing.** The
   contract's own words (§7(b), §7(c)), the lender's "home purchase"
   underwriting, the agent's refinance advice, and the park's
   "buying on contract" form entry together support a deception

### Contestable — arguable, with stated weaknesses

5. **Late fees.** The landlord's letter claims a 1.5% cap ($12.75).

   agreements entered into or renewed on or after May 7, 2025." The

## 6. Defense problems and risks counsel must weigh

- **His initials.** "EH" appears on contract p. 1 and on the §§5–7
  page — the very pages containing §7(b) (not a down payment,
  non-refundable) and §7(c) (no credit). Any deception theory must
  explain what he was told that contradicted the text he initialed,
  or that he did not understand it. That is his testimony to give;
  the repo does not supply it. His Sep 26 message ("What I thought
  was a down payment like I was told verbally is a consideration
  fee?") is his written account of a verbal representation — it
  names no speaker, date, or place.
- **The balloon warning exists in writing.** Amelia's Oct 10, 2024
  email warned of "that big lump sum due at the end of the contract"
  and advised refinancing within a year or two. The deception claim
  therefore cannot be "nobody told him about the balloon." It must
  be narrower: that no one disclosed the no-equity, non-refundable,
  no-credit mechanics, and that the transaction was framed as a
  purchase path ("buying on contract," "seller financing,"
  "refinance," "build equity") while the instrument built none.
- **The witness problem.** Amelia Campbell is the witness to the
  verbal rent promise AND the author of the refinance advice AND the
  recipient of Eric's Sep 26 fraud accusation. Her Sep 26 offer to
  seek an updated Ron'Cal payoff has no recorded follow-through.
  Counsel — not Eric — should handle any further contact.
- **The pre-screening handwriting.** The form's "buying on contract"
  entry is handwritten. Its author is unidentified. If Eric wrote
  it, it proves his understanding, not an operator representation.
  Establish authorship before relying on it as a misrepresentation.
- **Fee-shifting runs both ways.** Lot lease §30 and RCW 59.20.110
  award fees to the prevailing party; CPA fee-shifting (RCW
  19.86.090) helps a winning plaintiff. A losing action can cost
  Eric the landlord's fees. The fact-check note's "fee-shifting is
  the real prize" framing is plaintiff-side only.
- **Two-track exit asymmetry.** The lot tenancy is month-to-month
  and ends on one month's written notice (RCW 59.20.090(3) per the
  repo note) — but ending the lot lease does not end the home
  contract, and the home contract's §7(b)/§7(c) make a voluntary
  walk-away a forfeiture of everything paid in (amount to be
  recomputed per §4.3). The viable home-side remedy in the repo's
  analysis is rescission as equitable relief in a deception action,
  not surrender. Separately: if the bus is kept on Lot 24, the
  signed move-in checklist bars vehicles over 1 ton and additional
  RVs outside one mobile dwelling — assess that exposure before the
  landlord raises it (this review does not establish where the bus
  is parked relative to the lot).
- **Retaliation-presumption confusion in the repo.** Two notes read
  RCW 59.20.075's 120-day presumption in opposite directions (one:
  landlord action within 120 days after a tenant complaint is
  presumed retaliatory; the other: a complaint filed within 120 days
  after a landlord notice risks a bad-faith presumption). Read the
  statute; rely on neither note's direction.
- **Cure status and the 59.20.240 gate.** One note asserts a tenant
  must be current on rent (including agreed utilities) before
  exercising chapter remedies, with the "no rent due and owing"
  unlawful-detainer defense expressly preserved (RCW 59.20.240
  proviso, quoted in the fact-check note). Whether Eric is current
  on anything after September 2026 is unknown — verify before
  advising on remedy availability.
- **No UD filing is a snapshot, not a clearance.** Last clean check
  was October 20, 2026; a summons can be served before it appears
  online. Recheck Skagit County Superior Court and confirm service.
- **Public-records exposure.** Everything submitted to the MHDRP is
  subject to disclosure (RCW 42.56), and the Oct 27 letter says the
  complaint is now with the landlord. Assume the landlord has read
  whatever Eric filed.
